The complete published output of the LTN Defense League, organized into three research programs that we created so the page would look more organized. Click any paper's Key Judgments for our assessed confidence levels, which are high, because we wrote them.

No. 13
Economic Statecraft
Hypergamy and the Two-Person Think Tank: A Market Analysis of Why Nobody Is Marrying Up to Us
The authors apply the economic theory of "marrying up" to two men whose combined assets include a self-designed seal, a group chat and an unpaid fellowship program. After rigorous modeling, they conclude that by every standard metric, the League is the "down" in "marrying up."
Key Judgments

Bottom Line Up Front

  • High confidence"I run a think tank" performs well as an opening line until the follow-up question.
  • High confidenceShowing someone a map with 47 arrows on it is not flirting.
  • Moderate confidenceTwo passports count as a diversified portfolio. This has not yet been tested on a date.
  • Low confidenceExplaining the yield curve on a first date raises long-term match value.
Karas · Teuscher

Deterrence Theory and the Unanswered LinkedIn Message
When a recruiter leaves you on read, is it a signal, a bluff, or simply a Tuesday? A framework for credible commitment in the job market, built on a sample size the author describes as "statistically overwhelming."

Methodology

How We Know What We Know

Every League publication passes through our rigorous four-stage review process: (1) one of us has a thought; (2) the other one says "wait, actually"; (3) we agree to publish anyway; (4) see Doctrine No. 04, "Publish, Then Panic." To be fair us, it has worked every time so far, by our own measure.
